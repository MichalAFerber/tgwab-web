# The Forgotten Internet Tools of the '90s

Before the browser was the internet, the internet was a pile of protocols that each did one job. Three of them still sit in my head whenever someone calls a search bar "how humans find things."

## Archie — find the file

Archie was a search engine for **FTP**. You asked it the name of a file, it told you which anonymous FTP servers had it. That was the whole product: an index of what people had already put on ftp.somewhere.edu.

No crawl of "content." No ads. No knowledge panel. If you didn't know the filename, you were already in a forum asking a human. That constraint made you precise. It also meant a lot of the net was invisible unless someone had named the file like a sane person.

Every time I `find` or `fd` a box now, that's Archie at home. Every time a vendor hides a driver behind a JavaScript maze, I miss FTP.

## Gopher — the menu, not the page

Gopher was a nested menu of resources. Directories, text files, searches, links to other gopherspace. It was information architecture as a protocol. The web won because it was messier and could hold a magazine layout. Gopher lost because it refused to be a magazine.

I still think in Gopher when I build a start page or an intranet index: a list of places, one sentence each, no hero video. The [developer directory blueprint](/2026-06-02-one-page-developer-resource-directory/) is Gopher with CSS.

You can still find Gopher holes if you want. That's not the point. The point is that "everything is a document with seventeen trackers" was a choice, not a law of nature.

## BBSes — a place, not a feed

Before you had an always-on line, you had a phone number, a modem, and a board that was *somewhere*. One sysop. Message bases. File sections. Door games. You called after 9 p.m. because the line was the house line.

A BBS was a community with a door. The web replaced the door with a mall. I am not going to pretend FidoNet was kinder than Twitter on a technical level — people were still people — but the *scale* of harm was different because the room was smaller and someone owned the hardware.

Ham radio later scratched the same itch: a protocol, a license, a person on the other end. KJ4DIA is not a BBS. It is the same need.

## What I kept

- Indexes should be boring and complete.
- Menus beat infinite scroll when you actually want to go somewhere.
- A community with a sysop beats a platform with a trust and safety PDF.

Archie, Gopher, and BBSes didn't die because they were stupid. They died because a browser could pretend to be all three at once. I still reach for the older shape whenever the new one is wasting my time.
