# FixDNS: An Hour on Zoom, Not Another Checker

[IP Cow](/2026-07-23-ipcow-since-2005/) will look up your SPF, DMARC, DKIM, MX, and MTA-STS and tell you what is wrong. That is a tool. Most people who book me already know something is wrong. They cannot *publish* the fix in GoDaddy's panel while MailerLite is waiting on a CNAME, without dropping mail.

[FixDNS.net](https://fixdns.net) (and [brokedns.com](https://brokedns.com), same door) is the hour. Not a SaaS. Not a ticket queue. Me, on Zoom, US East Coast English, you still holding the passwords.

## The job

DMARC failing. SPF over the ten-lookup limit. DKIM selector the newsletter platform issued and nobody pasted. Google / Yahoo / Microsoft 2024 bulk-sender rules turning a list that "always worked" into spam. MX pointing at a host the registrar forgot during a Squarespace transfer. "I don't even know what's wrong" — honestly the most common booking.

I am a [MailerLite Certified Expert](/2024-01-21-mailerlite-expert/). That is seven academy certs, not a badge I bought. Same comfort with Mailchimp, Kit, Beehiiv, ActiveCampaign, Brevo, Klaviyo, SendGrid, Postmark, SES, Resend. If the platform hands you DNS records to verify, we can put them in.

Registrars are all the same underneath and all different in the click path: Cloudflare, GoDaddy, Namecheap, Route 53, IONOS, the Google Domains hangover at Squarespace, Porkbun, the rest of the long tail. I have been inside those panels for a living. The DNS is not the hard part. The hard part is not breaking the website while we fix the mail.

## How the hour actually runs

1. Book a slot on [Cal.com](https://cal.com/techguywithabeard/meet). Stripe at booking. $85 for the hour, $95 if you want the recording, $145 after hours.
2. Zoom. You share the screen, or you grant remote control. You watch every change.
3. I never take a password. I never accept a password. When the call ends, my access ends.
4. You leave with it working and a plain-English note of what changed. If you paid for the recording, the link is in the inbox after.

About 90% finish in well under the hour. If it genuinely needs more, we book another hour. No surprise invoice, no retainer, no "I'll have my team follow up." There is no team.

If we hop on and I cannot help, you get the money back. That is rare. Most things that look like DNS really are DNS.

## Why this exists next to the tools

I already shipped the checkers. SPF lookup, DMARC policy, DKIM key, the ten-lookup trap, deliverability in one shot — they are free on IP Cow. A small business with a newsletter does not fail because they needed another JSON endpoint. They fail because the TXT record went in the wrong zone, or the CNAME was on `www` instead of the root, or someone enabled DMARC at `p=reject` on Tuesday with no reporting window.

The [recovery-email](/2026-07-09-recovery-email-architecture/) post is how *I* own mailboxes. This post is how I will sit with *you* in a panel I do not have a login to.

I am retired from the full-time MSP chair. I do not want your tenant. I want the one broken zone and an honest hour. That is the whole business model, and it is why the site looks like a booking page instead of a funnel.

## What I will not do

I will not log into your registrar "real quick" after the call. I will not store your DNS host credentials "for next time." I will not run a monthly monitoring SKU that emails you a PDF. If you want monitoring, IP Cow is building that as a product. FixDNS is the human.

Book the hour if mail is on fire. Use the checkers first if you just want to know whether the record exists. Both are the job. Only one of them needs me on the call.
